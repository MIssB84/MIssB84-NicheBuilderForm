This recap covers the key takeaways from the June live call, in case you missed it!

Key Takeaways

Getting Started with Print on Demand

  • Start with one listing and build from there -- you don't need 20 or 50 ready before you launch. The goal is to open the shop, learn the process, and keep adding.
  • Pick a product type you can make over and over again without burning out. Narrowing your focus early helps you get better faster and keeps the process manageable.
  • Use your existing PNG designs to get started -- since PNGs are digital files and POD listings are physical products, selling the same design in both shops is completely fine.
  • Expect the first setup to take time, especially in Printify. Block out a full weekend to learn the workflow rather than assuming it will take an hour.
  • Having multiple print providers available for your product type is critical -- if one runs out of stock, you can switch to another without canceling orders.
  • Consider starting with a platform like Printify or Gelato. Printify works well for US and Canada; Gelato is a strong option if you want to sell worldwide, including Europe and Australia.

Choosing Your Product Type

  • Check the Bestsellers and Insights tabs inside Printify before committing to a product -- they show you what is actually printing in high volume right now, which is more reliable than guessing.
  • For sweatshirts, the Gildan 18000 is a top seller with 19 print providers, meaning you are unlikely to run into stock issues even during Q4.
  • The Comfort Colors 1566 sweatshirt has received complaints about fit and feel from some customers, so research it thoroughly before committing to it as your main product.
  • Rolled posters and matte canvas prints are currently showing as bestsellers on Printify, making them worth exploring if you want to branch into wall art as a physical product.
  • Unexpected products like all-over print socks are trending on Printify right now -- products with less competition can be worth testing even if they seem unconventional.
  • Now is an ideal time to start a POD shop if Q4 is your target -- it takes time to build inventory, warm up listings, and learn the process before the holiday rush hits.

Mockups That Convert

  • Do your mockup research by searching your product type on Etsy and studying pages one through three. The mockups you see repeatedly on bestseller and popular now badges are the ones that convert.
  • Focus on buying mockups you actually see working in the market rather than buying in bulk and ending up with dozens you never use. A handful of proven mockups will outperform a huge library.
  • For apparel, have both a flatlay and a lifestyle model mockup for every design, then AB test them as separate listings posted at the same time to see what your audience responds to.
  • If a mockup has distracting elements you don't love, use Canva to crop in closer or use Magic Edit to remove or replace them.
  • Reliable mockup shops to explore include Moonlight Mockups, Goldie Mock, Can Can Art Mockups, Lola Lucky Mockups, Mox Studio, and Mock City Studio.
  • Adding a short listing video makes a real difference. You can use the model video mockups from Lucky Lola, or create a quick AI-generated video in Kittl using a simple prompt like "model is happy and loves her shirt."

Setting Up Listings in Printify

  • Always start new listings from inside Printify and push them to Etsy rather than creating them directly in Etsy. Setting listings up in Printify first means orders process automatically without extra manual work on the back end.
  • When pushing a listing from Printify to Etsy, set it to Hidden so it lands in your drafts. Then go into Etsy drafts, remove Printify's default mockups, and add your own before publishing.
  • After your first product is set up in Printify, duplicate it for each new design. Update the image, title, and tags, then push to Etsy as hidden. This makes the process much faster after the initial setup.
  • Set your Etsy listings to automatic renewal and select your shop category before publishing. Also make sure to add your print production partner in the listing settings, which Etsy requires for POD shops.
  • Process orders manually rather than letting them auto-process immediately. This gives you a window to catch cancellation requests or errors before production begins. A daily phone reminder helps build the habit.
  • Use a credit card, not a debit card, to pay for Printify orders. It protects your money if anything goes wrong and lets you earn points on every purchase.

Pricing Your Products

  • Use a pricing calculator like the one in eRank or Everbee to figure out your numbers before you publish anything. Know your profit margin clearly and make sure your listed price is one you are comfortable with as a sale price.
  • A loss leader variant is important for getting clicks. Offer one low-priced variant, such as a small white, to pull shoppers into the listing. Every other size and color is priced normally.
  • Do not offer a blank shirt as your loss leader variant. Shoppers clicked on your listing because of the design, so a plain shirt as a cheap option feels misleading and is not a good customer experience.
  • Research competitor pricing by looking at pages one and two of your product search on Etsy. Note the prices on bestseller and popular now listings to understand what customers are already willing to pay.
  • Avoid pricing sweatshirts too high. Etsy shoppers are price-aware and overpriced items tend not to sell regardless of quality.
  • Once you settle on your base prices, leave them alone. Changing the base price on a listing notifies shoppers who have it in their cart that the price changed, and many will remove it rather than buy.

Managing Orders and Customer Issues

  • Printify handles reprints and refunds for damaged items, misprints, and lost packages at no cost to you. Submit the issue directly inside the order in Printify, select the problem type, and choose a reprint or refund.
  • When a customer reports a problem, ask them for a photo of the issue before contacting Printify. Having that evidence ready makes the resolution process faster.
  • Set clear expectations in your listing FAQ. Include a size chart, shipping and processing times, a note that all sizes are unisex, and a policy that you do not accept returns for sizing issues since you provide measurements upfront.
  • When your shop is brand new, consider absorbing the cost of a size-related refund rather than risk a bad review. A five-dollar loss early on is worth protecting your shop's reputation while you are building it.
  • Customers who experience issues and receive good service often leave positive reviews specifically mentioning how you handled the problem. Responsive communication goes a long way.
  • Offer between four and eight color options per listing. Too many choices cause decision paralysis and shoppers click away. Avoid listing colors that are very similar to each other for the same reason.

Running Etsy Ads

  • Do not turn ads on until you have around 25 to 50 listings in your shop. You need enough inventory and enough data about what is working before ads will be useful.
  • Commit to running ads for a minimum of 30 days before drawing conclusions. Etsy uses the first few weeks to figure out who to show your listings to, so early results are not representative.
  • A budget of one to two dollars a day is too low to generate meaningful data and will likely result in wasted spend. Start at a minimum of three dollars a day and increase as your results improve.
  • The conservative approach is to turn ads on only for listings that are already getting organic traction. The more aggressive approach is to run ads on everything and turn off the ones that spend without converting.
  • Check your ads twice a day in the first month, once in the morning and once at night. Over time as you understand what is working, you can pull back to weekly reviews.
  • Inside your ads dashboard, you can see which keywords Etsy is using to show each listing to shoppers. Turn off any keywords that are not relevant to what you are selling. This keeps your spend targeted and reduces wasted clicks.

Managing Multiple Shops

  • Two different shops with different product types will behave differently with ads and organic traffic. Treat them as completely separate businesses rather than comparing their performance directly.
  • Having more than one shop means that when one slows down, the other may be picking up. This makes your overall income more stable and keeps you from putting all your energy into a single point of failure.
  • Frame TV art is a strong option for wall art sellers because it has less competition than standard digital wall art, is faster to mock up, and has proven demand based on real sales data.
  • Do not mix digital and physical products in the same Etsy shop. It creates a confusing customer experience and logistical problems when someone buys what they think is a physical item and receives a digital file.
  • Keep your day job while building your Etsy shops. The goal is to reach a point where leaving is a choice, not a necessity. Having that option without needing to act on it immediately gives you a sense of control that changes how you operate.

Using AI Tools for Etsy

  • AI tools like ChatGPT and Claude are useful for generating design ideas and brainstorming quotes, but they do not have access to keyword tools like Everbee or eRank and do not know Etsy's algorithm nuances.
  • Do not use AI to write your titles and tags. It will give you logical-sounding suggestions that are not backed by actual search volume or competition data, which is what determines whether a keyword is worth using.
  • If AI suggests you go back and edit a well-performing title or tags, ignore it. Changing the title or tags on a listing resets its position in Etsy's algorithm, which can hurt momentum even if the listing recovers eventually.
  • Use proven keywords from real bestselling listings on Etsy rather than AI-generated ones. Pull keywords from multiple top-performing listings and combine them to create titles that are grounded in what is actually working.
  • The most effective way to use AI for Etsy is to give it context about your shop, your niche, and specific constraints, then use its output as a starting point you refine with your own knowledge and data.

Shop Strategy and Mindset

  • Let your data tell you what to make more of. Look at which listings get favorites and views and which designs are selling repeatedly, and lean into what your audience is already responding to.
  • AB test everything. Take one design, post it with a flatlay mockup as listing A and a lifestyle model mockup as listing B, publish both at the same time, and let the results tell you what converts better.
  • Pick a shop aesthetic that comes naturally to you. If you make content that reflects your genuine sense of humor or taste, it is easier to create consistently and you tend to connect more authentically with your target customer.
  • Keep your listing photo count between 8 and 10. Flooding a listing with 20 images gives shoppers too much to scroll through and increases the chance that something turns them off before they buy.
  • If your shop feels stuck, look at it objectively. Consider switching up your mockups, trying a new color variation, or testing a larger sale percentage for a period before assuming the designs are the problem.
  • Etsy is a numbers game but quality matters more than quantity when you are starting out. One well-researched listing with strong mockups, good SEO, and competitive pricing will outperform 50 rushed listings.
